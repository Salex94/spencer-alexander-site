Spencer Alexander Lawyers is a Box Hill, Melbourne law firm preparing enduring powers of attorney and appointments of medical treatment decision makers for adults across Victoria, and acting at VCAT when an attorney's conduct is questioned, with the principal overseeing every matter.

Who we act for

When people call us about a power of attorney.

People call when they are putting their affairs in order, often alongside a will, and when something has changed: a diagnosis, an operation, a move into care, a long trip, or a parent who is finding decisions harder. We act for the person making the documents, for family members who will be asked to act, and for attorneys who want to understand what the role requires of them.

Many people make these documents together with a will, as part of an estate plan, because a will speaks only after death, and these documents decide who acts for you while you are alive.

How we help

What we prepare, and how we help.

Enduring powers of attorney

We advise on whom to appoint for financial matters and for personal matters, whether your attorneys must act together or may act alone or by majority, who steps in as an alternative, when the power should start and any conditions or instructions you want to give. We check that each person you choose is eligible to act, and prepare the document in the form the Act prescribes.

Medical treatment decision makers

A medical treatment decision maker you appoint makes medical treatment decisions for you when you do not have capacity to make them, and must make the decision they reasonably believe you would have made. You can list more than one person in order, and the first who is reasonably available, willing and able to decide acts. We also draft advance care directives, which record your own instructions or values about treatment.

Acting as an attorney

If you have been appointed, we explain what the Act requires of you, including to act honestly, diligently and in good faith with reasonable skill and care, to avoid conflicts of interest unless they have been authorised, and to keep accurate records and accounts. An attorney for financial matters must also keep their own property separate from the person's, apart from property they own together, and where the person cannot decide, an attorney must give all practicable and appropriate effect to their wishes.

When an attorney may be misusing a power

VCAT can order an attorney's accounts to be lodged and audited, and can revoke the power or an attorney's appointment, and VCAT or the Supreme Court can order an attorney to compensate the person for a loss caused by breaching the Act. The person, an attorney, the Public Advocate, the nearest relative or anyone VCAT accepts has a special interest in the person's affairs can apply, and after the person's death, the executor or administrator of their estate can also apply for compensation. We act on either side of those applications, and our guide to misuse of a power of attorney explains how they work.

Time limits

When timing matters.

No deadline applies to making these documents, but capacity sets one in practice. You must be an adult with the capacity to make each document when you sign it, and each witness must certify that you appeared to have it. The law presumes capacity unless there is evidence otherwise and judges it decision by decision, so a diagnosis such as early dementia does not by itself rule out a power of attorney, but delay can.

Where no power was made in time, your family may need to ask VCAT for an administrator for financial matters or a guardian for personal ones, and VCAT makes an order only if it is satisfied, among other things, that the person needs one. Medical treatment is different, because where nobody has been appointed to make treatment decisions, the law names a decision maker from a list headed by the person's spouse or domestic partner.

Once the person who made the power, or the attorney, has died, an application for compensation for an attorney's breach must be made within six months after that death, or after the first death if both have died, although the Supreme Court or VCAT can extend that time. Good to know

Common questions about powers of attorney.

When does an enduring power of attorney start?

On the day it is made, unless the document says otherwise. You can choose a later time, circumstance or occasion instead, such as when you no longer have capacity for the matter, and if you lose capacity before then, your attorney can act anyway. The power is effective for each attorney only once they have signed a witnessed acceptance, and making it does not take away your own power to decide.

Who can I appoint as my attorney?

An adult who is not bankrupt or otherwise insolvent under administration and is not your paid care worker, your health provider or your accommodation provider. For financial matters, a person convicted or found guilty of an offence involving dishonesty can act only if they have told you and the document records it. You can appoint more than one attorney, decide whether they act together or alone, and name alternatives in case your first choice cannot act.

Is it too late if a parent has been diagnosed with dementia?

Not necessarily. The law presumes an adult has decision making capacity unless there is evidence otherwise, and capacity is judged decision by decision, so a diagnosis does not by itself rule out making a power of attorney. What matters is whether your parent can understand the information about the power and its effect, retain it, use or weigh it, and communicate a decision, and both witnesses must certify that your parent appeared to have that capacity. If it has gone, the family may need to apply to VCAT.

Who makes my medical treatment decisions if I have appointed no one?

Generally, the law names someone who is reasonably available, willing and able to decide. A guardian appointed by VCAT with power over medical treatment comes first. Otherwise it is the first of your spouse or domestic partner, your primary carer, an adult child, a parent and an adult sibling, in that order and taking the oldest where more than one fits, who has a close and continuing relationship with you. Appointing someone yourself lets you choose.
