Start here: rules and terms for the Chinese pages

This folder holds the Simplified Chinese versions of seven pages of spenceralexander.com.au, one document for each page, with the English and the Chinese side by side. None of them is live yet, and they go live only after you have checked them. This document sets out the rules and the terms used on every page, so please read it first.

Please check that every Chinese line says exactly what the English beside it says, with nothing added or left out on the law, that it reads naturally to a Mandarin reader in Melbourne, and that the legal terms are the ones clients would use and recognise. Change the Chinese column directly in Suggesting mode, or add a comment where you are unsure. When you have finished, tell Spencer, and he will ask Claude to apply your changes and publish the pages.

Two rules apply to every line. The site never uses dashes of any kind, including ——, and never uses brackets, including （）, apart from the phone number and the (Vic) and (Cth) in Act titles. And no line may describe the firm or anyone at it as a specialist or expert, 专家, or promise an outcome.

Please look at these first

  1. Every time limit on the home page and the practice pages: 12 months after a divorce order takes effect or two years after a de facto separation for property, six months after the grant for a family provision claim, and 21 days for a statutory demand. Each must say exactly what the English says, including that the court can extend or allow a late application.
  2. The fees page: every price, the words 含 GST, and every description of what a price covers and excludes must match the English exactly, because these are advertised prices.
  3. Nothing on any page may say or suggest that a lawyer at the firm speaks Mandarin. The pages say only 可提供普通话翻译. Please look closely at the contact page hero, which says a caller speaks directly with a lawyer and now adds that a Mandarin translator is available.
  4. VCAT is written 维多利亚州民事与行政法庭 VCAT. 审裁处 is the other common form. Please say which you prefer.
  5. Parenting terms use 育儿, such as 育儿安排 and 育儿令, rather than 子女抚养, which readers may confuse with 子女抚养费 for child support.
  6. Titles and descriptions for search engines add a few words the English does not have, such as 离婚律师, because those are what Mandarin readers search for. Each is noted in its row.
  7. Act titles are written without 《》 because the site uses no brackets of any kind. If 《家庭法》 reads much better to you, say so and it can be allowed for Act titles.
