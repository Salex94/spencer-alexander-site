# Writing and content review of spenceralexander.com.au

Prepared on Monday 9 November 2026 from the reports of eight reviewers, who between them read 52 pages. Nothing on the site has been changed.

The writing is accurate in the main and speaks throughout for the firm and its lawyers, and it is at its best in the 15 articles published since August 2026, which average 7.2 out of 10 for voice against 5.1 for the 21 published from March to July. Content and style are not yet the strongest they can be, because two FAQ answers go out of date tomorrow, 10 November 2026, about 30 other statements of law need correcting, confirming or completing, and the older articles still lean on slogans, label headings and closing sayings. Even the newest pages often leave out the fixed fee, the service page or the call prompt at the moment a reader decides, which makes persuasion their weakest score, and nearly every fix below changes words, links or schema rather than the design, with five pages already at the standard the rest should reach.

## How to read this report

The reviewers scored each page out of 10 for voice, clarity, persuasion and how quickly it answers the reader's question, ran the style checker on it, and graded each finding high, medium or low. Quotations from the site are exact apart from one thing. This report uses no brackets, so the phone number is written 03 9125 8355, the Vic and Cth after an Act's year are left off, official titles that contain brackets are written without them and subsection numbers are written in words, although the pages themselves keep their own forms. Every proposed wording speaks for the firm and its lawyers, never for Spencer personally, and anything that states law should be read against the authorised legislation and checked a second time before it is published.

## The 20 most valuable changes

These 20 changes cover all 19 findings the reviewers graded high, grouped where one fix serves several pages, and then the six medium findings that matter most. They are ranked by value, with every high severity change first.

### 1. Two FAQ answers on the 2026 intervention order changes go out of date tomorrow

**Severity:** high. **Page:** `faq.html`, the answers under "Are Victoria's intervention order laws changing?" and "What if I have been wrongly named as the respondent on an intervention order?", and the FAQ schema that repeats them.

**Current text:**

> Further changes, requiring police and the courts to look at the whole relationship when deciding whether the wrong person has been named as the respondent, and stopping an application being made against a child under 12, start on a day to be proclaimed and no later than 10 November 2026. The protections available now are unchanged, and an order can still be sought urgently.
>
> Victorian reforms passed in 2026 will require police deciding on a safety notice, and courts deciding whether to make an intervention order, to consider as far as practicable whether this has occurred, from a day to be proclaimed and no later than 10 November 2026.

**Problem:** The reviewer read section 2 of the Justice Legislation Amendment Family Violence, Stalking and Other Matters Act 2026, No. 1 of 2026, as made. Subsection 3 commences Division 3 of Part 2, which holds the misidentification and minimum age changes, on proclamation, and subsection 4 commences on 10 November 2026 anything not proclaimed by then. From tomorrow at the latest, the question, the future tense and "The protections available now are unchanged" are wrong on the page and in the schema that search results quote, and a person who has been wrongly named is told that a protection they already have does not yet apply.

**Proposed text,** once the accuracy routine has confirmed the commencement date in the authorised version, using the proclaimed date instead if Division 3 was proclaimed earlier:

> How did Victoria's intervention order laws change in 2026?
>
> Further changes came into operation on 10 November 2026, so police and the courts must now look at the whole relationship when deciding whether the wrong person has been named as the respondent, and an application can no longer be made against a child under 12. An order can still be sought urgently.
>
> Since 10 November 2026, police deciding on a safety notice, and courts deciding whether to make an intervention order, must consider as far as practicable whether this has occurred.

The same Division also deals with the default duration of final orders and continuing protection for children, which the answer does not mention, so the accuracy routine should decide whether it needs a sentence. Make the same changes in the FAQ schema, and move the FAQ's Last reviewed date, its schema dateModified, the sitemap lastmod and the llms.txt Last updated line together. Once this is done, the respondent section of the intervention orders article can link the wrongly named answer, as the legal queries below set out.

### 2. Property after separation says the tax and duty concessions are unavailable, against its own correction

**Severity:** high. **Page:** `insight-property-after-separation.html`, the myths paragraph and an FAQ answer that is also in the schema.

**Current text:**

> Until formalised by consent orders or a binding financial agreement, nothing is final: the claim survives, the tax and duty concessions are unavailable, and future windfalls remain in play.

and in the FAQ answer, "The claim survives, the tax and duty concessions are unavailable, and future windfalls remain in play."

**Problem:** On 29 September 2026 the page's consent orders paragraph was corrected against subsection 1 of section 44 of the Duties Act 2000, which needs no court order, so a transfer under an informal deal is harder to bring within the duty exemption rather than shut out of it. The myths paragraph and the FAQ still say the concession is unavailable, so the page contradicts itself, and its full verification is still due. **Proposed text,** reusing the verified sentences of `insight-agreed-property-settlement.html`, for the second check:

Victoria's duty exemption for a transfer made solely because of the breakdown of a marriage or domestic relationship depends on the State Revenue Office being satisfied that is why it was made, and orders or a binding agreement are the clearest way to show it.

### 3. De facto separation: the definition tells a separated but still married reader the opposite of the law

**Severity:** high. **Page:** `insight-de-facto-separation.html`.

**Current text:**

> Under the Family Law Act, you are in a de facto relationship if you are not married or related by family, and you have a relationship as a couple living together on a genuine domestic basis.

**Problem:** The reviewer read section 4AA of the Family Law Act 1975, compilation 101. It requires that the two people are "not legally married to each other", and says a de facto relationship "can exist even if one of the persons is legally married to someone else or in another de facto relationship". The phrase "you are not married" reads as not married to anyone, so a reader who has separated but not divorced, and now lives with a new partner, is told they cannot be in a de facto relationship when they can.

**Proposed text,** in the Act's own words and subject to the second check:

> Under the Family Law Act, you are in a de facto relationship if the two of you are not legally married to each other or related by family, and you have a relationship as a couple living together on a genuine domestic basis. That can be so even if one of you is still legally married to someone else.
