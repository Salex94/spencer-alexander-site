## What to bring to the first call

The will or a copy of it, the death certificate if it has arrived, any letters from banks or super
funds, and questions. These are documents an executor or next of kin plainly holds. No legal
requirement to hold or bring anything is stated. "Keep the original will safe, exactly as you found
it" is practical guidance matching the executor checklist; rule 2.04(2)(d)(i) is why the original
matters.

## Generalised or not verified

- Court procedure is described only as the Rules set it out. Court sites were not read, and no
  processing time is stated.
- Joint tenancy, tenants in common and renunciation after intermeddling are general law, stated with
  "generally" and consistent with the site's articles.
- The sale of land needing a grant is stated as "generally", from practice and faq.html; the
  Transfer of Land Act 1958 was not read.
- Section 99A(1), (2), (3)(b) and (4), which protect maintenance distributions, distributions made
  after written consent, and distributions after a notice of intended claim lapses at three months,
  are left out for length. The page states only the no notice limb, as a sufficient condition.
- No court fee figure, and no indexed figure, appears. The $250,000 band is confirmed in item 4.2(a).

## Wrong in law elsewhere, for Spencer

- wills-and-estates.html urgent strip: "an estate can be distributed once that period passes"
  leaves out that the executor's protection depends on having no notice of a claim, s 99A(3), and
  that a late claim cannot undo an earlier distribution, s 99(4). Today's audit, item 13, has the
  corrected text.
- wills-and-estates.html In brief: "witnessed by two adults" states an age requirement the Wills Act
  does not impose. This is today's audit item 8; I did not re-read the Wills Act.
- fees.html: "nil for an estate worth less than $250,000". Item 4.2(a) turns on the gross value of
  the estate, so an estate with gross assets over $250,000 pays a fee even if its net worth is less.
  "whose gross value is less than $250,000" is accurate.
- faq.html, probate cost answer: "a smaller charge for publishing the notice" is not smaller for an
  estate under $250,000 gross, where the filing fee is nil and the notice fee under item 4.1(a) still
  applies. A minor imprecision.
