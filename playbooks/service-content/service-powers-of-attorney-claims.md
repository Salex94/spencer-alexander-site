## Legal statements on the page

| Where | Statement | Authority read |
|---|---|---|
| Lead, brief bullet 1, FAQ 1 | An enduring power of attorney lets someone you trust make financial decisions, personal decisions or both, and can be used from the day it is made unless it says otherwise. | POA ss 22(1), (2) and 39(1), (2). |
| Lead, brief bullet 2 | It keeps working if you lose capacity, and making one does not take away your own power to decide. | POA ss 22(3) and 42. |
| Urgent strip, time limits | It can be made only by an adult who has the capacity to make it. | POA s 23(1)(a), (b); MTPD s 26(1) for the medical appointment. The strip's "while they can still understand what they are signing" follows the audit's verified W9 wording. |
| Brief bullet 3, medical block | A power made today does not cover medical treatment; that needs an appointed medical treatment decision maker. | POA s 3, definition of personal matter, which excludes medical treatment; MTPD s 26. "Made today" because MTPD s 103 and POA s 155 keep medical powers in some older instruments. |
| Brief bullet 4, process step 2 | Two adult witnesses, one a doctor or a person authorised to witness affidavits, who sign in front of the person and each other and certify that the person appeared to sign freely and to have capacity. | POA ss 33(1)(b), 35(1)(b), 35(2)(a), 36(1)(a); MTPD s 36(1)(a) to (c) and (2)(a), (b), with s 3, definition of authorised witness. "In front of" includes presence by audio visual link under the remote witnessing procedure, POA ss 5A and 33(4), which the page does not describe. |
| Process step 2 | For a power of attorney, neither witness can be an attorney under it, the person's relative, a relative of an attorney, the person's care worker or accommodation provider, or a person signing at the person's direction; for a medical treatment appointment, neither can be a person it appoints. | POA s 35(2)(b) to (d); MTPD s 36(2)(d) and s 37(b). Every limb of s 35(2) is stated. Following the Act's words, the page says "an attorney under it" and does not extend the bar to an alternative attorney, which the accuracy routine settled on 27 October 2026. |
| Process step 3 | Each attorney signs a witnessed acceptance before the power is effective for them; each medical treatment decision maker accepts in writing, witnessed by an adult, before the appointment comes into effect. | POA ss 37(1) and 38(1); MTPD ss 28(1)(g) and 29. |

| FAQ 2 | Eligibility: an adult, not insolvent under administration, not the person's paid care worker, health provider or accommodation provider, and for financial matters a person convicted or found guilty of an offence involving dishonesty only if disclosed and recorded. | POA s 28(1)(a) to (d), with s 3, definitions of care worker and offence involving dishonesty. Every limb is stated. Trustee companies, s 28(2), are left out so no condition goes missing. |
| Medical block | An appointed decision maker decides when the person lacks capacity, must make the decision they reasonably believe the person would have made, and more than one can be listed in order, the first reasonably available, willing and able acting. | MTPD ss 26, 28(1)(e), 28(2), 55(1), 58(1) and 61(1). |

| Acting as an attorney block | Duties: honesty, diligence and good faith, reasonable skill and care, avoiding conflicts unless authorised, accurate records and accounts; for financial matters, keeping property separate apart from joint property; giving all practicable and appropriate effect to the person's wishes when they cannot decide. | POA ss 21(2)(a), 63(1)(a), (b), (d), (f), 66 and 69(1), (2). Introduced with "including", so the list is not presented as complete. |
| Misuse block | VCAT can order accounts lodged and audited and revoke the power or an attorney's appointment; VCAT or the Supreme Court can order compensation; who can apply. | POA ss 77(1), 78, 116(1)(e), (f), 120(1)(a), (b) and 122(1). Every applicant category in s 122(1) is named, and s 78(c) adds the executor or administrator for compensation, added in the final audit of 9 Nov 2026. |
| Time limits | The law presumes capacity unless there is evidence otherwise and judges it decision by decision; a diagnosis does not by itself rule out a power. | POA s 4(2) and (4)(a); MTPD s 4(2) and (4)(a). ## Generalised or left out, and why

- Supportive attorney appointments, POA Part 7, were read but left out to keep the page to the documents on the fees page.
- Revocation was moved off the page when the questions were cut to five; it is the spare question below.
- The remote witnessing procedure, POA s 5A, is not described; the page's "in front of" is true of it.
- No statement that an attorney must notify anyone before acting, POA s 40, because it depends on what the instrument says.
- Practice statements, not law: people usually call when something has changed, many make these documents with a will, and we act on either side of VCAT applications.
- What to have with you names an earlier power or appointment, the current will and the names of the people being considered, all things a person planning these documents plainly holds or knows, states no legal requirement to bring anything, and keeps the line that having little on paper is fine.

## The brief asked for six questions; the gate requires five

The task brief asked for six FAQs. `playbooks/service-pages.md` and the gate's "service pages meet the playbook standard" check, which the central pass of 9 November 2026 left unchanged apart from the page limit, require exactly five, so the page carries five. If the limit on questions is raised, this spare question was read against the Act today and can be added as the fifth, before the cost question:

- Q: Can I change or cancel my enduring power of attorney?
- A: Yes, while you have the capacity to make one giving the same power. A revocation must be made in the prescribed form and signed and witnessed in the same way as the power itself, and you must take reasonable steps to tell your attorneys. A later enduring power of attorney also revokes an earlier one unless the later one says otherwise.
- Authority: POA ss 44, 45, 46, 48, 50(1) and 55.
