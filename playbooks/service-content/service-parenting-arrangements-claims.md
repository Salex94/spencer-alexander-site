## Advice and practice statements, not law

- "You should not, without the other parent's agreement or a court order" move the children in FAQ 4 is advice, not a statement that the Act requires consent. Without orders the Act encourages consultation under s 61CA and requires joint decision only where an order provides for it under s 61DAA.
- "Moving first and asking later can seriously damage your case" repeats faq.html. It is a statement about litigation practice, not verified against any provision.
- The situations people call about, the documents to bring, and the office facts are practice facts taken from the family hub and CLAUDE.md. The list of documents names only what a parent in this position plainly holds and states no requirement to bring anything.

## Generalised, and why

- FAQ 2 says "a risk of either" for family violence and child abuse. The Act's child abuse limb, s 60I(9)(b)(ii), is a risk of abuse if there were a delay in applying, and each limb in (b) refers to a party to the proceedings. The page presents the grounds as a partial list, so the shorter wording is kept for readability.
- The best interests matters in s 60CC(2) and (3) are not listed in full. FAQ 1 says the court weighs "the matters the Act lists", names only the first, safety, and does not present it as the whole test. The matching article sets them out.
- Section 60CC(4), which lets the court consider consent orders without applying every best interests matter, is not stated.
- The pre-action notice in FAQ 3 is required where there is no family dispute resolution service, a party fails or refuses to take part, or no agreement is reached. "If that fails" covers all three.
- Orders to stop a child being taken out of Australia are described in general terms. Sections 67ZD and 68B are cited, and the criminal offence in s 65Z, which has a family violence exception in s 65Z(2), is left out.
- Changing final orders is named as a service but not explained. Section 65DAAA was read, and the page states nothing about its test.
- Court procedure comes from the Rules only. The court's own website is blocked from this environment and was not read.
