## Legal statements on the page

| # | Statement, where it appears | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Married couples generally have 12 months from the divorce order taking effect. Appears in the lead, urgent strip, time limits section and FAQ 1 and 2. | s 44(3)(a), (c). "Generally" covers nullity, s 44(3A) and (3B), and the exclusion of s 79A and variation proceedings. |
| 2 | De facto partners have two years from the end of the relationship, or from separation. Appears in the lead, urgent strip, time limits section and FAQ 2. | s 44(5)(a)(i). "Generally" covers s 44(5)(a)(ii). |
| 3 | After the deadline, a claim needs both people's consent or the court's leave, and leave is not guaranteed. Appears in the urgent strip, time limits section and FAQ 2. | s 44(3), 44(5)(b) and 44(6). This follows the audit's correction: consent or leave, never leave alone. |
| 4 | Leave for a property claim is granted only if hardship would otherwise be caused. After a marriage, the hardship is to either spouse or a child. After a de facto relationship, it is to the person applying or a child. | s 44(4)(a) and 44(6)(a). The maintenance limbs, s 44(4)(b) and (6)(b), are left out because the page speaks of a property claim. |
| 5 | The clock starts at the divorce, not at separation, so a couple long separated but never divorced are not out of time. | s 44(3): the limit applies only once a divorce order has taken effect. |
| 6 | Filing before the deadline means you need neither consent nor leave. | s 44(3) and (5)(a). |
| 7 | There is no automatic equal split. The court weighs contributions and current and future circumstances, and makes orders only if it is satisfied they are just and equitable. | s 79(2) to (5) and s 90SM(2) to (5). The Act contains no presumption of equal shares. "No automatic equal split" is an inference from that text. |
| 8 | The court considers the effect of family violence, and of intentional or reckless wastage, which the 2025 reforms added. | s 79(4)(ca), 79(5)(a) and 79(5)(d), with s 90SM(4)(ca), (5)(a) and (5)(d). These came from Act No. 118, 2024, in force 10 June 2025. |
| 9 | Contributions include those made as a parent or homemaker. | s 79(4)(c) and s 90SM(4)(c). |
| 10 | A private agreement does not stop either person applying to the court later. Consent orders or a binding financial agreement make an agreed settlement binding. | s 79(1) and s 90SM(1) are unaffected by an informal agreement. Also s 71A, s 90G(1) and s 90UJ(1). "Binding" is used plainly: orders can still be set aside under s 79A or s 90SN, and agreements under s 90K or s 90UM. |
| 11 | For de facto partners, property orders can generally be made only if one of four conditions is met: the relationship lasted at least two years; there is a child of the relationship; the relationship was registered; or the applicant made substantial contributions and refusal would cause them serious injustice. | s 90SB(a) to (d). "Generally" covers the geographical requirement in s 90SK. |
| 12 | Married couples can settle property before or without a divorce. De facto partners can settle once the relationship has ended. | For married couples, s 4(1) "matrimonial cause" para (ca)(i) and s 79(1). For de facto partners, s 90SM(1): "after the breakdown". |
| 13 | Superannuation is treated as property. It can be split by court order or by a binding financial agreement. | s 90XC(1) and (2). Splitting by order is s 90XT. Splitting by agreement is a superannuation agreement within a financial agreement, ss 90XH, 90XHA and 90XJ, in Part VIIIB. |
| 14 | For consent orders that split super, the fund must be notified at least 28 days before filing, unless it consents sooner in writing. | Rules r 10.06(1) to (4). The underlying procedural fairness requirement is s 90XZD(1)(a). |
| 15 | Each person owes the other full and frank disclosure while preparing for a property case, and owes it to the court as well once a case starts. Appears in a service block, process step 1 and FAQ 4. | s 71B(1), (2), (5) and (6), and s 90RI(1), (2), (5) and (6). |
| 16 | The court can order disclosure, take a failure into account and order costs. | The notes to s 71B(2) and s 90RI(2), which list those powers. The notes are explanatory and are worded "might", so the page says "can". |
| 17 | The court can set aside orders obtained through a failure to disclose. | s 79A(1)(a) and s 90SN(1)(a), which require a miscarriage of justice by reason of suppression of evidence, including failure to disclose. |
| 18 | The court's rules expect a schedule of assets, income and liabilities, and key documents, to be exchanged early. | Rules Schedule 1, Part 1, cl 4(1) and (2). The page says "expect" where the Rules say "must". |
| 19 | Before a contested application, the rules expect dispute resolution and then a written offer, where it is safe, unless an exception such as urgency or family violence applies. | Rules r 4.01(1) to (3), and Schedule 1, Part 1, cl 3(1) to (5). The written offer is the notice of intention to start a proceeding, with a genuine offer. |
| 20 | An agreement reached outside court goes to the court as an application for consent orders. Whether agreed or decided at a hearing, orders are made only if the court is satisfied they are just and equitable. | Rules r 10.04(4) and 10.07(1). Also s 79(2) and s 90SM(2), which apply to consent orders. |
| 21 | Each person needs independent legal advice before signing a binding financial agreement, which is one of the steps the Act sets for it to be binding. | s 90G(1)(b) and s 90UJ(1)(b). The wording says "one of the steps" because s 90G(1A) and s 90UJ(1A) allow a court declaration where a step is missed. |
| 22 | For consent orders, the law requires no lawyer. | Rules r 10.04(2): a draft is signed "by each party or the party's legal representative". Nothing requires representation. |
| 23 | A binding financial agreement can be drafted, or advice given on one drafted elsewhere. | No legal claim is made. It matches the firm's service as fees.html and the BFA article describe it. |

## Site pages I believe are wrong in law

- **family-law.html, FAQ "Is there a time limit on property settlement?"**
  - It gives the court's permission as the only way to apply late, and omits both people's consent.
  - The consent route is in s 44(3) and 44(5)(b).
- **faq.html, "Is there a time limit for a property settlement after separation?"**
  - Same omission of consent, s 44(3) and 44(5)(b).
  - My page states the corrected law, so its FAQ 2 departs from both pages on purpose.
- **family-law.html, In brief**
  - It says "within 12 months of a divorce order". It should say "taking effect", s 44(3)(c).
- **faq.html, de facto answer**
  - The substantial contributions route lacks its serious injustice limb, s 90SB(c)(ii).
- **fees.html, "What the prices do not include"**
  - It says the consent orders filing fee is "reduced" for card holders and people in hardship.
  - In fact card holders are exempt and hardship can be waived.
  - I did not read the Fees Regulations myself. This relies on today's audit.
- **resource-separation-first-30-days.html, section 6**, which is not in the audit
  - It says "After those windows close, you need the court's permission to apply".
  - It omits consent, s 44(3) and 44(5)(b).
  - This page links the checklist.
