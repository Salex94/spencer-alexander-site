## What to bring to the first call

A copy of the will if the caller has one, the grant if one has been made, letters from the executor, a claimant or their lawyers, and their questions, with the family hub's lines that little on paper is fine and the first conversation is about understanding the situation. No legal requirement to hold or bring anything is stated.

## Generalised, omitted or not verified

- Capacity, undue influence, knowledge and approval and fraudulent calumny are general law and were not verified against any case, since no case is cited. They are named only as grounds on which a will can be challenged.
- Court practice notes, the Supreme Court's General Civil Procedure Rules and court websites were not read; court and AustLII sites are blocked. Procedure is stated only as far as the Acts and Order 8 of the 2023 Rules set it out.
- Left out deliberately: the notice of intention to claim in APA s 99A(4); how costs are commonly ordered in practice; how long claims take; and any statement that most claims settle.
- Rule 36 of the Australian Solicitors' Conduct Rules was not read in an official current copy.

## Wording elsewhere on the site that is wrong or incomplete in law

- faq.html, "Is there a time limit to contest a will or make a family provision claim?" and "What is the difference between contesting a will and challenging its validity?": both state the six month limit without the extension rule in APA s 99(2) and (3).
- insight-contesting-a-will.html, "How long do I have to make a claim?": "executors are effectively protected if they distribute after the window closes" omits the no notice condition in APA s 99A(3).
